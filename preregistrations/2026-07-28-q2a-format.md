# Pre-registration #54: Q2_A — a dp4a-native 2-bit format at Q2_K quality

**Author:** Federico Sciuca · **Date staked:** 2026-07-28, BEFORE running the kernel. **STAKED.**

## The format, fixed before measuring

Asymmetric 2-bit, group 16, **byte-aligned** scale and min, superblock 256. **3.125 bits/weight.**

```
per 256-weight superblock: 64 B quants + 16 B scales + 16 B mins + fp16 d + fp16 dmin = 100 B
row plane layout (K=2048): [128 x u32 quants][128 B scales][128 B mins][8 fp16 d][8 fp16 dmin] = 800 B
```

One uint32 is exactly one group of 16 weights, **pre-permuted** (byte j carries weights
j, j+4, j+8, j+12) so `(v >> 2j) & 0x03030303` yields four *contiguous* weights straight into
dp4a's int8 lanes with one shift and one mask.

The asymmetry is free in the inner loop:
`sum((q*s - m)*x) = s * dp4a(q,x) - m * sum(x)`, and `sum(x)` is row-independent, so it is hoisted
into shared memory once per block. **Q2_K pays its asymmetry per row; this pays it once.**

## Why these numbers and not others

- **Quality is already settled** (`tools/kernelprobe/quality.py`, real dequantized weights, 12
  projection matrices): this exact scheme measures **0.995× Q2_K's reconstruction RMSE** — parity.
  Symmetric variants do not reach it at any group size, because with 4 levels you get a zero level
  or symmetry but not both.
- **Scale precision is irrelevant** — fp16 and byte-times-superblock give identical RMSE at every
  group size — so the byte-aligned form costs nothing in quality and removes all bit unpacking.
- The price is **3.125 vs Q2_K's 2.625 bits/weight: 19% more bytes.**

## Stakes — kernelprobe, 512 MB buffer, contiguous, this card

Measured reference points already on the ladder: 2.5-bit symmetric dp4a = **342.4 GW/s**;
4.5-bit dp4a = 231.2 GW/s. llama.cpp's Q2_K on a real 7B = **165.1 GW/s**.

- **P-1.** Q2_A lands in **270–330 GWeights/s** — below the 2.5-bit symmetric kernel (it reads 25%
  more bytes and does one more multiply per group) but well above the 4.5-bit kernel.
- **P-2 (THE CLAIM).** Q2_A is **≥ 1.6×** llama.cpp's measured Q2_K (165.1 GW/s), i.e. ≥ 264 GW/s,
  *at parity reconstruction quality*.
- **P-3.** Correctness: rel.err < 1e-3 against a double reference computed in logical weight order,
  which is what proves the permutation and the asymmetric reconstruction are both right.

## KILL RULE

**If P-2 fails — Q2_A is under 1.6× Q2_K — then the 2.07× headroom measured in #53 is not
reachable by a format that keeps Q2_K's quality**, the asymmetry and the extra 0.5 bits/weight eat
the win, and a new quant type is not worth the end-to-end integration cost. I would then report the
headroom as real but unclaimable rather than keep chasing it.

## What this still does not prove

A microbenchmark is not a model. Even at P-2, the end-to-end gain is diluted by attention, KV,
norms and sampling, and the MoE flagship additionally pays the gather penalty measured at 1.8× on
our own kernel. And reconstruction RMSE is a **proxy** for quality — perplexity is the real test and
has not been run. No end-to-end or quality claim is made here.

## Scored (2026-07-28)

**Verdict: P-1, P-2 and P-3 all HIT. Q2_A delivers 1.70× llama.cpp's Q2_K at parity reconstruction
quality, and the prediction band was right before the run.**

```
L1e sym2   (2.500 bit, dp4a)   108.4 GB/s   346.9 GW/s   rel.err 2.0e-08   1.15x WORSE quality
L1f Q2_A   (3.125 bit, dp4a)   109.7 GB/s   280.9 GW/s   rel.err 3.8e-04   0.995x quality (parity)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
llama.cpp Q2_K (2.625 bit), real 7B, all-in-VRAM        165.1 GW/s
```

| stake | threshold | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | 270–330 GW/s | **280.9** | HIT |
| P-2 | ≥ 1.6× Q2_K | **1.70×** | HIT |
| P-3 | rel.err < 1e-3 | 3.8e-04 | HIT |

**On P-3's error being 3.8e-04 rather than the ~1e-08 the other kernels show:** the host reference
multiplies by the exact `0.002f`/`0.003f` superblock constants while the kernel reads them back
through fp16, whose rounding is ~7.6e-05 relative. The discrepancy is the *reference* being
idealized, not the kernel being wrong — and it is the expected magnitude. Recorded rather than
quietly waved through, since a correctness check that is looser than its neighbours deserves an
explanation.

Note also that **GB/s is nearly identical for the 2.5-bit and 3.125-bit kernels (108.4 vs 109.7)**.
Both are ALU-bound, not memory-bound, so the extra 25% of bytes is essentially free and the whole
difference in GW/s is just the bits. That is a useful design fact: at this end of the ladder, buy
quality with bits — they are cheap — and never buy it with instructions.

## The end-to-end projection, stated as a projection

The microbenchmark is pure matvec; a real decode also runs attention, KV, norms and sampling.
#53 gives the calibration: llama.cpp's Q4_0 reaches **88.5%** of our 4.5-bit probe ceiling
end-to-end (204.7 of 231.2 GW/s). Applying the same factor to Q2_A's 280.9 gives ~249 GW/s, i.e.
**~32.7 tok/s for the 7B against Q2_K's measured 21.67 — about +51%.**

That is a projection with one calibration constant borrowed from a different format, **not a
measurement**, and it will not be claimed as one until Q2_A is integrated and run.

## What is now blocking, honestly

1. **Not integrated into llama.cpp.** 1.70× is a kernel-vs-kernel number. Integration is a real
   project — a new `ggml_type`, quantize/dequantize/vec_dot on CPU and CUDA, type_traits, GGUF
   ftype — and is the next commitment, not a footnote.
2. **Perplexity has not been run.** Reconstruction RMSE is a proxy. Parity in RMSE is necessary,
   not sufficient, and a real quality gate has to be perplexity on held-out text.
3. **Files are 19% larger** than Q2_K. For a user who chose 2-bit to fit VRAM at all, that can
   erase the benefit entirely — this is a speed-at-quality win, not a size win.
4. **One card.** The entire effect is that this GPU's ALU is weak relative to its bandwidth.
